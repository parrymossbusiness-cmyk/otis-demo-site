Know your number

Your number is the full monthly cost. It is not only the loan. Work it out before you look at a single house.

What a $500,000 house costs each month

Take this example. You buy a $500,000 house in Vallejo. You use an FHA loan with 3.5% down.

Change the numbers to fit the home you want.

The loanabout $3,100
Property taxabout $460
Home insuranceabout $100
Mortgage insuranceabout $220
Your total each monthabout $3,880

Split between two people: $1,940 each.

This is an estimate for planning only. It uses a 30-year loan, property tax at about 1.1% of the price, $100 a month for home insurance, and FHA mortgage insurance at about 0.55% of the loan each year, the same as the example above. The rate shown is an example. Use the rate your lender gives you.

That is everything, not only the loan. Rates change, so ask a lender to run your real numbers.

Your debts change your number

Lenders compare your debts to your income. It is called debt to income. A lower number is better.

Student loans do not stop you from buying a home. Lenders count your student loan payment each month. The rules differ by loan type, so ask your lender how yours is counted.

The bigger problem is credit cards and car loans. Each payment lowers what you can borrow.

Find the help

You may not need $50,000 saved to buy a house. Here is where the help comes from.

A state loan for the down payment

A state program called CalHFA can lend the down payment. It can cover 3% to 3.5% of the price. You pay that loan back later. There is no monthly payment on it. These programs have income limits. They can also run out of money.

Every East Bay program and what is open now

A VA loan with nothing down

If you served, a VA loan may need no down payment. That works if the price is not more than the appraised value. There is no monthly mortgage insurance. You may pay a one-time funding fee, and you must live in the home.

FHA and VA loans compared

Local help that opens and closes

Some programs give much more but open only in rounds or run out of money. CalHFA Dream For All can lend up to 20% for first-generation buyers. Antioch has its own program. Oakland's is out of money for now.

Down payment help by city

A credit from the seller

We ask the seller for a credit. Often it is 2.5% to 3% of the price. It can pay your loan fees, your appraisal and your title costs. Your savings stay in the bank. Your loan type sets how big a credit can be, so ask your lender for that limit early.

How much the seller can pay on each loan

A lower rate for the first two years

It is called a 2-1 buydown. In year one, your rate is 2% lower. In year two, it is 1% lower. The seller pays for it as a credit. In year three, the rate goes back up. So make sure you can afford the full payment.

How a buydown works in Antioch

A loan that pays for repairs

It is called an FHA 203(k) loan. It pays for the house and the repairs. You put 3.5% down. You get one loan and one payment. It takes more paperwork and an approved contractor, so give yourself extra time to close.

Fixing up an older home in Vallejo

A second unit that helps pay

FHA lets you buy 2 to 4 units with 3.5% down. You must live in one of the units. A lender can count 75% of the expected rent. A back unit in Richmond can rent for $1,600 to $2,000. Check the permits before you make an offer. Being a landlord is also work.

The appraiser checks the house too. Peeling paint can be flagged. So can a missing handrail or an old roof. The seller may need to fix those first.

Read the tax bill before you make an offer.

The city transfer tax

Oakland adds a 1.5% tax when a home sells. That covers homes from $300,000 to $2 million. On a $700,000 home, that is $10,500. The buyer and the seller often split it, so your half is about $5,250.

Some first-time buyers qualify for a lower city rate. It depends on your income. Check the rules with the City of Oakland.

Berkeley charges a 1.5% transfer tax on most homes. On an $800,000 home, that is $12,000. If you make the home safer in an earthquake, the city can refund up to one third of the tax. That is up to $4,000 back. You have one year after the sale, and the work has to qualify. Keep every permit and receipt. Check the rules with the City of Berkeley first.

The sewer pipe under the yard

It is called a private sewer lateral. The pipe must pass a test when the home sells. In Oakland, EBMUD runs that program. It also covers Alameda, Albany, Emeryville and Piedmont.

A new pipe can cost $5,000 to $12,000. A seller can pay a deposit and get 180 more days. Then the repair can land on the buyer. So we put it in your offer. Check your address with EBMUD before you offer.

Mello-Roos

It is an extra tax on newer neighborhoods. It pays for roads, parks and schools. A lender counts that tax against you. $400 a month can cut your loan by about $50,000.

Many homes south of Lone Tree Way in Antioch have it. Older homes in north Antioch often do not.

Fire insurance in the Berkeley hills

Homes near Grizzly Peak sit in high fire zones. Many insurers will not write a policy there. You may need the California FAIR Plan, with a second policy on top. Together they can cost $4,000 to $8,000 a year.

Near San Pablo Avenue, most homes are outside those zones. Check the fire map for any address first.

East Bay buyer questions answered fast.

What first-time home buyer programs can I use in the East Bay?

First-time buyers in the East Bay can use three main types of help. An FHA loan needs 3.5% down if your credit score is 580 or higher. A VA loan needs no down payment for eligible veterans and service members. CalHFA's MyHome program can add up to 3.5% of the price for your down payment or closing costs, with no monthly payment. Some programs, like CalHFA Dream For All and Alameda County's AC Boost, open only in rounds. Check that a round is open before you plan around one. How much do I need saved to buy my first home in the East Bay?

Many buyers think they need 20% down. An FHA loan needs 3.5% down. On a $500,000 home, that is $17,500. A state program called CalHFA can lend part of the down payment, and a seller credit can pay closing costs. These programs have income limits and can run out of money, so ask a lender which ones fit you.

Can the seller pay my closing costs?

You can ask the seller for a credit. Often it is 2.5% to 3% of the price. It can pay your loan fees, your appraisal and your title costs, so your savings stay in the bank. Your loan type sets how big a credit can be, so ask your lender for that limit early. See the limit for each loan.

Do student loans stop me from buying a home?

No. Lenders count your student loan payment each month and compare your debts to your income. The rules differ by loan type, so ask your lender how yours is counted. Credit card and car payments often hurt more, because each payment lowers what you can borrow.

What hidden costs should first-time buyers check in the East Bay?

Check four things before you write an offer. Oakland and Berkeley charge a city transfer tax. In the EBMUD area, the sewer pipe under the yard must pass a test when a home sells. Many newer homes in Antioch have a Mello-Roos tax. Homes in the Berkeley hills can cost much more to insure. Ask for the tax bill and get an insurance quote first.
