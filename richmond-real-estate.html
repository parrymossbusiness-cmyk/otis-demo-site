Buying your first home in Richmond

A ferry to San Francisco, BART from the start of the line and miles of shoreline. Here is what first-time buyers should know about Richmond.

Ferry to San Francisco in about 35 minutes

Life in Richmond

Getting to San Francisco
Ferry, about 35 minutesThe ferry leaves from Marina Bay and lands at the Ferry Building in downtown San Francisco. You can also take BART. Richmond station is the start of the line, so trains start empty. Amtrak's Capitol Corridor stops there too.
On foot
Richmond Annex and Point RichmondFrom the Richmond Annex, you can walk to El Cerrito Plaza BART and the shops at El Cerrito Plaza. Point Richmond has a small downtown with old brick buildings, coffee houses and bakeries.
Things to do
The Bay TrailMarina Bay sits by the water. Walk the Bay Trail there, or head to Keller Beach and the Miller/Knox Regional Shoreline past Point Richmond.

Areas first-time buyers look at

Richmond is more than 30 square miles, so the area you pick changes a lot. These are areas Otis walks with first-time buyers.

Next to El Cerrito

Richmond Annex

The Annex sits at the south edge of the city, next to El Cerrito and Albany. Streets are lined with 1940s and 1950s bungalows, and El Cerrito Plaza BART is a short walk.

Condos and townhouses by the water

Marina Bay

Marina Bay was built on the old Kaiser shipyard land. It has condos and townhouses around a harbor, with the ferry and the Bay Trail close.

A small historic downtown

Point Richmond

Point Richmond grew up in the early 1900s at the end of the Santa Fe Railroad. Hillside streets have Victorians, Craftsman bungalows and newer view homes.

Hillside and in-between streets

East Richmond Heights and the Panhandle Annex

These are two more areas Otis shows first-time buyers. You often get more space than in North Oakland.

Mid-century ranch homes

North and East

Between San Pablo Avenue and Interstate 80, you find single-story ranch homes and established yards.

Prices change a lot from street to street in Richmond. Decide what matters most to you first: BART, the water, or a lower price. Then walk each area on a weekend.

A weekend in Richmond

What a weekend can look like when you live in Richmond.

Rosie the Riveter visitor center

The Rosie the Riveter WWII Home Front National Historical Park has its visitor center at 1414 Harbour Way South, in the old Ford Assembly Building complex on the water. It tells the story of the people who built ships and planes in Richmond during World War II. Entry to the visitor center and its films is free.

The Bay Trail at Marina Bay

Paved Bay Trail paths wrap around Marina Bay and the harbor, close to the ferry terminal.

Keller Beach and Miller/Knox

Just past the tunnel from Point Richmond, Keller Beach and the Miller/Knox Regional Shoreline have a sandy beach, picnic spots and ridge trails with views of the bay.

The Plunge

Point Richmond's indoor public swimming pool has served swimmers since 1926.

Check these before you make an offer

The list price is not the value

In Richmond and El Cerrito, many homes are listed low to bring in more offers. The list price is only where the bidding starts. Check homes sold within half a mile in the last 60 days, with the same size and condition, and set your top number before you see other offers.

Down payment help in Contra Costa

Ask about silent second loans, which have no monthly payment, and matching grants for your savings. These can work with state loans from CalHFA. Your lender has to be approved for these programs, and programs open and close during the year. Start on the City of Richmond homebuyer page.

A second unit that helps pay

FHA lets you buy a place with 2 to 4 units with 3.5% down, if you live in one of them. A lender can count 75% of the expected rent. Check the permits before you make an offer. Being a landlord is also work.

Do this weekTake the ferry from Marina Bay once, then walk the Richmond Annex to El Cerrito Plaza BART. Compare the two rides before you choose.

Richmond questions answered fast

How long is the ferry from Richmond to San Francisco?

The ferry from Marina Bay to the Ferry Building in downtown San Francisco takes about 35 minutes. You can also take BART from Richmond station, the start of the line, so trains start empty. Ride the commute once at rush hour before you buy.

Where do first-time buyers look in Richmond?

Otis often starts first-time buyers in the Richmond Annex, then East Richmond Heights and the Panhandle Annex. The Annex is a short walk to El Cerrito Plaza BART. Marina Bay has condos and townhouses by the water. Prices change a lot from street to street.

Is there down payment help for Richmond buyers?

Yes, some programs help Contra Costa buyers. Ask about silent second loans with no monthly payment and matching grants for your savings. They can work with CalHFA loans. Your lender must be approved, and programs open and close, so start on the City of Richmond homebuyer page.

Why do Richmond homes sell over the list price?

Many homes are listed low to bring in more offers, so the list price is only where bidding starts. Look at homes sold within half a mile in the last 60 days instead. Set your top number before you see other offers, and write it down.
