Closing costs


Can the seller pay my closing costs?

How East Bay first-time buyers ask for a seller credit, how much each loan allows, and when sellers say yes.

Updated October 19, 2026 by Otis White Jr.

  • Limits by loan
  • What it pays
  • When sellers say yes
  • Questions
The short answer

Yes, in many cases. You ask for a seller credit in your offer, and the seller pays part of your closing costs, like loan fees, the appraisal and title. Your loan type sets the limit. FHA allows up to 6% of the price. Conventional loans allow 3% to 9%, based on your down payment. VA has no cap on normal closing costs, and a 4% cap on extras. The credit cannot be more than your actual costs.

How much each loan allows

The seller can only give up to your loan's limit. These limits are for a home you will live in.

LoanMost the seller can pay
FHA6% of the sales price
Conventional, less than 10% down3% of the price
Conventional, 10% up to 25% down6% of the price
Conventional, 25% or more down9% of the price
VANo cap on normal closing costs. Extras, like the funding fee, are capped at 4% of the home's value.

Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-4.1-02, VA closing costs and concessions and HUD's FHA rules. Checked October 19, 2026. Your lender has the final word.

What a seller credit can pay

Loan and title costs

Lender fees, the appraisal, title insurance and escrow fees. These are the costs most credits go to.

Prepaid costs

Your first months of property tax and home insurance, which you pay at closing.

A lower rate

The seller can pay for a 2-1 buydown. Your rate is 2% lower in year one and 1% lower in year two. How a buydown works.

City costs at closing

In Oakland, a credit can help with your share of the city transfer tax. See Oakland's costs.

Two rulesThe credit cannot be more than your actual closing costs, and you cannot get cash back from it. If you raise your offer to fund a credit, the home still has to appraise at that price.

When sellers say yes

The home has been for sale a while

A seller who has waited weeks is more open to a credit than one with several offers.

The inspection finds repairs

Ask for a credit instead of repairs. You close sooner, and you choose who does the work.

The offer is strong in other ways

A clean offer with a solid preapproval and a short timeline makes a credit easier to win.

New homes from a builder

Some builders in Fairfield pay part of your closing costs. You may have to use their lender, so compare one other loan. See Fairfield.

Seller credit questions answered fast

Can the seller pay my closing costs?

Yes, in many cases. You ask for a seller credit in your offer, and the seller pays part of your closing costs, like loan fees, the appraisal and title. Your loan type sets the limit. FHA allows up to 6% of the price. Conventional loans allow 3% to 9%, based on your down payment. VA has no cap on normal closing costs, and a 4% cap on extras. The credit cannot be more than your actual costs.

How much can a seller pay on an FHA loan?

Up to 6% of the sales price. The credit can pay closing costs, prepaid items and some loan fees. It cannot be more than your actual costs, and you cannot get cash back.

How much can a seller pay on a conventional loan?

For a home you will live in, the limit is 3% of the price if you put less than 10% down. It is 6% if you put 10% up to 25% down, and 9% if you put 25% or more down.

How much can a seller pay on a VA loan?

There is no cap on the seller paying your normal closing costs. Extras, like paying your VA funding fee or paying off a debt, are capped at 4% of the home's value.

How much do East Bay buyers usually ask for?

Otis often asks for a credit of 2.5% to 3% of the price. The right number depends on the home, how long it has been for sale and your loan's limit. Ask your lender for your limit before you write the offer.
